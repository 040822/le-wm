\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
Our evaluation addresses three questions: whether CoWM supports effective control, which training connections contribute to candidate evaluation and refinement, and how much decision time these capabilities require. We organize the study around system performance, coupling ablations, physical consequence diagnostics, inference choices, and success--latency trade-offs.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:eval_protocol}
\paragraph{Tasks and protocol.}
We evaluate TwoRoom, Push-T, Reacher, and OGBench-Cube using common frozen start--goal cohorts. Goal images are 25 primitive steps after the start, and the episode budget is 50 steps. The default prediction and execution horizons are both 25 steps (five blocks of five actions), subject to early termination. CoWM uses two Euler generation steps; Selection (P3) scores 64 candidates, and Refinement (P0-PO-L) applies two post-generation updates to one proposal, with step size 0.01 and RMS offset bound 0.2. The main conditions retain native environment action handling without additional candidate clipping. Selection$^{\dagger}$ changes only Reacher to 10-step execution and scoring while retaining the same checkpoint and 25-step generation. Its other three task cells reuse Selection results.

\paragraph{Baselines and comparisons.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} compares CoWM with LeWM using CEM~\cite{maes2026lewm}, LeFlow~\cite{huang2026leflow}, and Sub-JEPA. LeWM and Sub-JEPA use 300 CEM candidates, 30 iterations, and 30 elites. LeFlow uses 64 candidates and 16 flow steps with its paired frozen LeWM backbone. Baselines retain their native history interfaces and 25-step execution and scoring horizons. The completed scope is \PaperResult{protocol-completed-scope}. Same-actor scorer controls and training-coupling contrasts are separate studies.

\paragraph{Metrics and uncertainty.}
Success is reported as the mean and sample standard deviation ($\mathrm{ddof}=1$) across evaluation seeds 42, 100, 2026, 3407, 1234, and 4444. For the four-task average, we first average tasks within each seed and then compute the mean and standard deviation across seeds. These error bars measure evaluation-seed variation, not training-seed uncertainty or confidence intervals. Timing uses \PaperResult{appendix-timing-scope}. Table~\ref{tab:main_results} averages the four task mean latencies equally; it measures runtime-warmed initial replanning, not cached steady-state MPC or total episode time. Blue TBD fields denote remaining evidence in this working draft.

CoWM-Selection achieves 96.25\% average success, compared with 96.17\% for LeFlow, 85.17\% for Sub-JEPA, and 79.92\% for LeWM. Its mean initial replanning latency is 29.88\,ms, versus 51.43\,ms for LeFlow and 625.67\,ms for LeWM (ratios of 1.72 and 20.94, respectively). The 0.08 percentage-point success difference from LeFlow is descriptive and does not establish superiority. Refinement reaches 94.50\% at 45.23\,ms, below Selection's success at higher latency. Selection$^{\dagger}$ raises Reacher success from 88.33\% to 100.00\% and the four-task average to 99.17\%, but changes the control horizon. More frequent replanning can increase episode computation even though its mean initial call costs 29.53\,ms. These system comparisons do not isolate a training-coupling effect.

\subsection{Contribution of training coupling}
\label{sec:training_results}
We distinguish parameter sharing, action-input exposure, and the gradient through the action input. \emph{Separate-Recorded} trains two DiT backbones using recorded actions for B; \emph{Shared-Recorded} shares the DiT with the same supervision. Their encoder-sharing setting and capacity are reported explicitly. \emph{Shared-Pred-Detach} mixes A estimates into B but stops their gradient, while \emph{Shared-Pred-Full} retains it. \subsection{Candidate evaluation and executed refinement}
\label{sec:ranking}
\paragraph{Common-pool ranking.}
For each restored state, we generate a fixed pool of physical action sequences and execute each sequence to obtain its true task outcome. Every scorer receives the same pool. Pool coverage measures whether any candidate succeeds; selected success measures whether the scorer finds a successful candidate. For a task-specific physical cost $c$, regret is $c(\mathbf a^{\rm selected})-\min_i c(\mathbf a^{(i)})$. We additionally report Spearman correlation between predicted and physical costs. Pool identity, horizon, and valid-state counts are \PaperResult{ranking-pool-scope}.

\paragraph{Physical effect of refinement.}
PO starts from identical proposals for each model. An equal-RMS random perturbation controls for the size of the action change after legal action handling. GF is compared with its unguided generation trajectory using the same initial noise. We report true cost decrease, the fraction of improved states, and the fraction where a predicted improvement worsens the true outcome. A single-step GF update that equals PO is not counted as independent evidence. The physical effect is \PaperResult{guidance-real-effect}, and the corresponding closed-loop effect is \PaperResult{guidance-closed-loop-effect}.

\paragraph{Physical readouts.}
Frozen linear and MLP readouts measure which physical quantities can be recovered from observed latents. The main Cube analysis covers eight robot, end-effector, gripper, and block attributes; matched auxiliary probes cover TwoRoom, Push-T, and Reacher. These readouts do not measure future-state prediction quality, and they do not establish that a scorer ranks or refines actions well. Full observed-latent results and target definitions are in the supplement; predicted-latent evaluation remains a separate extension.
