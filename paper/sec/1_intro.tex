\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Latent world models support visual goal-conditioned control by predicting candidate outcomes. DINO-WM and LeWM search for actions that move future representations toward an image goal~\cite{zhou2024dinowm,maes2026lewm}.

Repeated prediction and search contribute to decision cost. A learned action generator can supply reusable proposals, while a world model selects or refines them using their predicted consequences.

Fast LeWorldModel predicts action-prefix futures in parallel~\cite{gao2026fastlewm}. LeFlow learns latent proposals for a frozen world model~\cite{huang2026leflow}; FlowMPC combines flow proposals with planning~\cite{hamel2026flowmpc}.

These interfaces raise a training question: does learning proposals and dynamics together improve decisions under a limited computation budget?

Answering it requires separating training effects from the value of querying an existing predictor.

CoWM shares a visual encoder and diffusion transformer between goal-conditioned action flow and parallel latent prediction.

It exposes three connections: shared parameters, predicted-action inputs, and prediction gradients through those inputs.

Sharing may transfer useful features, but the objectives can also interfere. Estimated actions still receive recorded future targets; this training objective does not supply their actual counterfactual outcomes.

At inference, CoWM directly executes an action proposal, selects among proposals, or refines them through a learned goal cost. These alternatives permit a measured trade-off between success and decision time.

We assess training variants on common physical actions and restored simulator states. Candidate coverage, top-1 selection, physical regret, and executed refinement distinguish available good actions from the ability to choose them.

The fixed-checkpoint reference finds a modest mean selection gain and faster inference than large-budget CEM, while missing its task-level non-inferiority gate. The matched-training comparison estimates \PaperResult{intro-main-effect}.

The final interpretation is \PaperResult{intro-evidence-scope}. We report task and training-seed variation, including absent or reversed effects, rather than infer control gains from prediction loss or continuous ranking alone.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A coupled action--dynamics formulation that distinguishes shared parameters, estimated-action inputs, and the action-mediated gradient path.
\item A decision evaluation connecting candidate coverage, physical consequence ranking, local refinement, and closed-loop success.
\item A comparison of success and measured inference cost against independent prediction and search, with training contrasts interpreted within their actual scope.
\end{itemize}
