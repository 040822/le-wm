\subsection{Visual control under a decision budget}
Given a current observation $o_0$ and goal image $o_g$, the controller proposes an action sequence $\mathbf a=a_{0:H-1}$, executes a configured prefix, and replans from a new observation. Each $a_h$ contains $k$ primitive environment actions. We define the decision budget as the time available for one complete policy call, including observation encoding, proposal generation, consequence prediction, selection, and any refinement. Candidate count $N$, flow integration steps $S$, and refinement updates $K$ determine how this budget is allocated. Our objective is to support useful action decisions with a limited number of model queries and updates.

An offline training window contains $(o_0,a_0,o_1,\ldots,a_{H-1},o_H)$. A visual encoder and projector $f_\phi$ map observations to $z_h=f_\phi(o_h)\in\mathbb R^d$. During training, $z_g=z_H$; at deployment, $z_g=f_\phi(o_g)$. A shared diffusion transformer (DiT) $T_\theta$ implements action generation (A) and dynamics prediction (B), using mode-specific token projections, output heads, and attention masks. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes their training connections and decision interfaces.

\subsection{Action generation and parallel latent dynamics}
\paragraph{Goal-conditioned action generation.}
We train the action mode with conditional flow matching~\cite{lipman2022flow}. For recorded actions $\mathbf a$, Gaussian noise $\epsilon$, and interpolation time $\tau\sim\mathcal U[0,1]$, define
\begin{equation}
 x_\tau=(1-\tau)\epsilon+\tau\mathbf a,\qquad v^\star=\mathbf a-\epsilon.
\end{equation}
The generator predicts the action velocity $v_\theta(x_\tau,\tau;z_0,z_g)$ and minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_A=\mathbb E\left[\operatorname{mse}\left(v_\theta(x_\tau,\tau;z_0,z_g),v^\star\right)\right].
 \label{eq:action_loss}
\end{equation}
Action tokens attend to one another and to current and goal anchors; anchor queries do not attend to noisy actions. At inference, $S$ Euler steps integrate the velocity field from noise to an action proposal, denoted $A_\theta(z_0,z_g;\epsilon,S)$.

\paragraph{Parallel action-conditioned prediction.}
The dynamics mode receives the interleaved sequence $[z_0,a_0,q_1,a_1,q_2,\ldots,a_{H-1},q_H]$, where $q_h$ is a future-latent query. A lower-triangular attention mask lets $q_h$ access $z_0$ and the action prefix $a_{0:h-1}$ while excluding later actions. All future latents are predicted in one transformer evaluation:
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\mathbf z}_{1:H}=B_\theta(z_0,\mathbf a;s).
 \label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
Here $s$ is a training condition specified below. B receives no goal input: it predicts action consequences, which are subsequently compared with the goal. Parallel prefix prediction~\cite{gao2026fastlewm} removes recursive model calls across the imagined horizon. Candidate batching provides an additional, separate source of parallel execution.

\subsection{Three forms of training coupling}
\label{sec:coupling}
\paragraph{Parameter-sharing coupling.}
A and B reuse the DiT backbone and visual representation. Their losses jointly update the shared parameters, including when B is trained exclusively on recorded actions. This implements both tasks in one model; its parameter, training, and decision costs are compared with a separate-DiT control in our resource evaluation.

\paragraph{Action-input coupling.}
The action mode provides a clean-action estimate from its training-time velocity:
\begin{equation}
 \tilde{\mathbf a}=x_\tau+(1-\tau)v_\theta(x_\tau,\tau;z_0,z_g).
 \label{eq:clean}
\end{equation}
For a Bernoulli mask $m\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_e)$, the dynamics input is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf a^{\rm in}=(1-m)\mathbf a+m\tilde{\mathbf a}.
\end{equation}
This mixture exposes B to estimates produced by A as well as recorded actions, motivated by the difference between recorded-action supervision and generated candidates at deployment. Training estimates need not have the same distribution as fully sampled inference proposals. We use $s=1$ for recorded inputs and $s=\tau$ for estimated inputs, with the weighted latent objective
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_B=\mathbb E\left[w(s)\operatorname{mse}\left(B_\theta(z_0,\mathbf a^{\rm in};s),\mathbf z_{1:H}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:dynamics_loss}
\end{equation}
The mixture schedule and weighting are given in the supplement. Both input branches use the recorded future embeddings as targets. Thus, for an estimate that differs from the recorded action, this objective couples generation to the recorded transition without observing the estimate's counterfactual outcome. Its decision value is assessed through actual executed consequences.

\paragraph{Action-mediated gradient coupling.}
We retain differentiability of $\tilde{\mathbf a}$ so that $\mathcal L_B$ reaches the action-generation computation through B's action input. For shared DiT parameters, the two routes are
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_\theta\mathcal L_B=
 \left.\frac{\partial\mathcal L_B}{\partial\theta}\right|_{\tilde{\mathbf a}\ \mathrm{fixed}}
 +\frac{\partial\mathcal L_B}{\partial\tilde{\mathbf a}}
   \frac{\partial\tilde{\mathbf a}}{\partial\theta}.
 \label{eq:gradient_routes}
\end{equation}
The first term updates shared parameters through B. The second lets the future prediction objective participate in learning A. Replacing $\tilde{\mathbf a}$ by $\operatorname{sg}(\tilde{\mathbf a})$ removes only the second route; parameter sharing remains. The full objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_A+\lambda_B\mathcal L_B+\lambda_R\mathcal L_{\rm SIGReg},
 \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
where SIGReg follows LeWM~\cite{maes2026lewm}. The encoder is jointly trained, with gradients retained through the embedding targets. The three connections are evaluated through recorded-input, detached-input, and separate-backbone controls.

\subsection{Fast action selection and refinement}
\label{sec:planning}
For fixed $z_0,z_g$, define a differentiable terminal goal cost
\begin{equation}
 J(\mathbf a)=\operatorname{mse}\left(B_\theta(z_0,\mathbf a;1)_H,z_g\right).
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
This learned cost supplies both candidate scores and action gradients. Model parameters remain fixed during decision-making.

\paragraph{Candidate selection.}
We sample $N$ proposals with independent noise, $\mathbf a^{(i)}=A_\theta(z_0,z_g;\epsilon_i,S)$, evaluate their predicted futures in a batch, and select
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf a^\star=\mathbf a^{(i^\star)},\qquad
 i^\star=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{1\leq i\leq N}J(\mathbf a^{(i)}).
\end{equation}
This procedure uses learned proposals followed by one scoring stage, avoiding iterative resampling. We call it P3 in the experiments. Direct execution of one proposal without B is P0 and provides a minimal-computation reference.

\paragraph{Post-generation refinement (PO).}
Starting from a generated proposal $\mathbf a^0$, we perform $K$ gradient updates in normalized action coordinates:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 g^j&=\nabla_{\mathbf a}J(\mathbf a^j),\\
 \mathbf a^{j+1}&=\Pi_{\mathcal B_R(\mathbf a^0)}\left(
 \mathbf a^j-\eta\frac{g^j}{\max(\operatorname{RMS}(g^j),\varepsilon)}\right).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:po}
\end{equation}
The trust region $\mathcal B_R$ bounds RMS displacement from the original proposal; physical action bounds are enforced separately. PO can refine a single proposal or the candidate selected by P3. Refining every candidate before selection is a distinct, more expensive configuration.

\paragraph{Guidance during generation (GF).}
After an Euler step reaches time $u$ and state $x$, we form $\hat{\mathbf a}(x,u)=x+(1-u)v_\theta(x,u;z_0,z_g)$. We differentiate $J(\hat{\mathbf a}(x,u))$ with respect to $x$ and apply bounded normalized updates before continuing generation. Each update uses the current Euler proposal as its local reference. For $S=1$, $u=1$ and $\hat{\mathbf a}=x$, so matched PO and GF updates coincide mathematically. Their implementations can still have different runtimes; multi-step guidance is evaluated separately.

\paragraph{Allocating decision computation.}
Selection requires $S$ batched generation rounds and a B scoring stage; PO adds $K$ B forward/backward updates, while GF interleaves generation and cost-based updates. Increasing $N$, $S$, or $K$ changes both computation and decision behavior. We compare these choices against random-initialized CEM (P1) and actor-initialized CEM (P2), using measured decision latency to account for batch size, horizon, precision, and hardware. The selected or refined action prefix is executed before replanning.
