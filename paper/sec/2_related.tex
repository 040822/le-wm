\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Latent World Models for Visual Planning.}
Latent world models support visual planning by predicting the consequences of candidate actions in a learned representation space. DINO-WM predicts pretrained visual features and searches for actions that bring predicted futures toward an image goal~\cite{zhou2024dinowm}. LeWM jointly learns representations and dynamics from pixels using latent prediction and a Gaussian-distribution regularizer; its physical probes also examine information recoverable from the learned latents~\cite{maes2026lewm}. Fast LeWorldModel replaces recursive rollout with parallel prediction conditioned on action prefixes~\cite{gao2026fastlewm}. CoWM builds on these latent prediction principles and couples the predictor with a learned action generator, studying how their training connections affect decisions made using predicted futures.

\paragraph{Generative Models for Planning.}
Learning trajectory proposals can amortize part of the search required by world-model planning. Diffusion Policy illustrates generative action-sequence modeling~\cite{chi2023diffusion}, while flow matching provides a framework for training conditional generators~\cite{lipman2022flow}. Within latent world-model planning, LeFlow learns a flow-based trajectory prior, decodes latent transitions through inverse dynamics, and ranks the resulting candidates using a frozen world model~\cite{huang2026leflow}. FlowMPC uses a flow policy to propose actions for world-model-based MPPI planning~\cite{hamel2026flowmpc}. CoWM instead learns direct action proposals together with a parallel latent predictor and uses predicted consequences for candidate selection or bounded refinement. We study how this joint training affects the resulting planning interface and its success--latency trade-off.

\paragraph{Joint Learning of Dynamics and Actions.}
Joint models connect action learning with future prediction through shared representations and training objectives. UWM couples action and video diffusion in a unified transformer, using independent diffusion timesteps to support policy and dynamics inference~\cite{zhu2025uwm}. FLARE combines action flow matching with future latent representation alignment, focusing on policy co-training and leaving planning as a potential extension~\cite{zheng2025flare}. Fast-WAM studies the contributions of video co-training and test-time future generation~\cite{yuan2026fastwam}, while Faster-WAM retains future conditioning through efficient video--action interaction~\cite{zhao2026fasterwam}. Beyond action generation, $\tau_0$-WM uses predicted futures to evaluate and rectify proposals~\cite{zhou2026tauwm}. These works motivate distinguishing the training benefits of future prediction from its use during inference. CoWM examines this distinction in a compact, image-goal-conditioned latent model by separating parameter-sharing coupling, action-input coupling, and action-mediated gradient coupling. Its controlled comparisons ask which connections improve candidate ranking and action refinement under a limited decision budget.
