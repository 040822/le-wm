\begin{abstract}
Latent world models enable visual planning without reconstructing future images, but repeated candidate evaluation and iterative search can still make action decisions expensive. We investigate whether jointly learning action generation and action-conditioned dynamics can improve candidate selection and refinement under a limited decision-time budget. We propose CoWM, a coupled latent world model that uses a shared diffusion transformer for goal-conditioned action generation and parallel prediction of future representations. CoWM connects the two tasks through shared parameters, generated-action inputs to the dynamics model, and prediction gradients propagated through those inputs. At inference, the model generates action proposals, evaluates their predicted consequences in parallel, and supports gradient-based refinement either after or during generation. Candidate count, generation steps, and refinement updates control the allocation of decision computation. Our evaluation examines closed-loop success, candidate ranking, executed refinement, and measured decision latency, with matched training contrasts to assess the role of coupling. Across four tasks, CoWM selection achieves 96.25\% average success with 29.88\,ms mean initial replanning latency, compared with 96.17\% and 51.43\,ms for LeFlow at the declared operating points. These measurements use one checkpoint per method and task and six evaluation seeds; matched-budget and training-coupling studies remain pending. These comparisons determine when learned proposals and their predicted consequences can reduce reliance on extensive online search.
\end{abstract}
