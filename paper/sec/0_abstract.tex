\begin{abstract}
Learning action proposals can reduce world-model search, but joint action--dynamics training need not improve decisions.

We study this question with CoWM, a shared visual encoder and transformer for action flow and parallel latent prediction.

We distinguish parameter sharing, predicted-action inputs, and the action-mediated gradient path. Evaluation separates proposal coverage, consequence ranking, executed refinement, and the success--latency trade-off.

A four-task fixed-checkpoint reference shows a 2.0-point mean selection gain over matched random choice. Gains depend on task; frozen-representation interventions do not identify a reliable training-coupling benefit.
