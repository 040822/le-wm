\section{Implementation and Reproducibility}
\label{app:implementation}
This supplement separates measured reference results, interim matched-training checks, and conditional mechanism contrasts. Internal paths and hashes are maintained in the drafting guide, outside the anonymous manuscript.

\paragraph{Architecture and training.}
The R4-AB implementation uses a randomly initialized ViT-Tiny encoder with patch size 14, latent width 192, and six shared transformer blocks. Each block has six heads and MLP width 768.

The matched configuration uses batch size 128, BF16, AdamW at learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-3}$, and gradient clipping at 1.0.

Actual training visits, updates, and checkpoint histories are \PaperResult{appendix-training-history}.

Unused C/D/E extensions remain allocated in the implementation. A/B-only objectives do not remove their storage. Resource reporting distinguishes active compute from total resident parameters.

\paragraph{Coupling and controls.}
The main draft uses Separate-Recorded and Shared-Recorded for the architecture controls. ``Recorded'' specifies B's action input, not a ground-truth dynamics oracle. Historical Recorded-clean corresponds to shared recorded-action training with time one and unit weights. Recorded-control retains Full's timestep/weight schedule. These are distinct variants of recorded-action supervision, and only matched conditioning and weighting isolate an input-source effect.

At fitting time, estimated-action probability is $\min(e/10,0.5)$ for effective epoch $e$. The sample weight is $w(s)=\min(s/0.2,1)$, with $\lambda_B=1$ and $\lambda_R=0.09$. The inherited validation loss instead uses probability one; validation and fitting losses do not have the same input mixture.

Recorded-control keeps the Joint arm's mask, B timestep, and sample weight while replacing B inputs with recorded actions. Recorded-clean uses recorded actions, time one, and unit weights; B-only also uses clean recorded-action supervision.

Same-architecture arms share an initial state, episode split, training normalizer, and paired window schedule.

Independent LeWM has a different architecture and history window, so matched update counts do not imply identical training examples or compute.

\section{Decision and Readout Details}
\label{app:diagnostics}
\paragraph{Candidate pools.}
Coverage means that at least one sampled candidate succeeds; top-1 success is the outcome selected by a scorer. Success and physical-distance oracles need not select the same action.

For state $s$, candidate set $\mathcal A_s$, and executed physical distance $d_s$, regret is
\begin{equation}
r_s=d_s\!\left(\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{a\in\mathcal A_s}J(a)\right)
-\min_{a\in\mathcal A_s}d_s(a).
\end{equation}
Distance units and termination validity are recorded per task. A pool oracle bounds that pool at its diagnostic horizon, not a long-horizon optimal policy. State-level resampling preserves dependence among candidates.

\paragraph{Physical readouts.}
Readout train/validation splits exclude diagnostic trajectories. Ridge and small-MLP fits, with a random-encoder reference, test observed-feature readability. The fitted readout is frozen before application to predicted latents.

The current Cube target is block position. Additional physical quantities are reported only when measured with defined fields, units, and matched splits. Heterogeneous physical errors are not averaged as one common scale.

\paragraph{Refinement.}
PO comparisons use a common initial action. GF uses matched initial noise and the corresponding unguided trajectory. After legal projection, the random control matches actual action displacement. Valid state counts, early termination, and predicted-improvement/physical-degradation rates accompany the result.

\section{Online Data Extension}
\label{app:online}
The exploratory online branch freezes an epoch-2 actor and representation, then fine-tunes scorers with real simulator branches. It changes the supervision setting and is separate from the offline main study.

The locked Joint-B rank arm has P3 success of 18.5\% versus LeWM's 10.0\% on Push-T (two-task Holm $p=0.012$). Reacher gives 15.0\% versus 11.0\% (Holm $p=0.291$), each on 200 locked starts.

Against the original Joint-B, the changes are +4.5 and +4.0 points, with intervals crossing zero. The significant Push-T comparison against LeWM therefore does not alone establish an online gain over the original Joint model.

Extra-update offline controls and both scorers' online arms are reported together. Secondary comparisons do not inherit the primary test's correction, and this cohort is not reused to choose a different online arm.

\section{Timing and Final Reporting}
\label{app:reporting}
Isolated timing includes encoding, generation, conversion, scoring, backward passes, and search. Device work is synchronized; hardware, precision, goal caching, threads, warm-up, measurement counts, and interference checks are recorded.
